### Complexity
What then is Complexity Science?  
Well... there is not really a single definition I would say. Some people will say that there is not a definition at all. Personally, I like to have a definition that I base my modelling efforts on. It makes things transparent and falsifiable to some degree - even though that is a topic on its own. Especially complex systems are basically everywhere so it seems rather hard to falsify their presence. But in general it makes things more practical if there is a working-definition at least.  
  
**My working definition:** OK..., so primarily system's micro-interactions scale to something that can be and is often fundamentally different at the macro level than the system's component's micro-structure and interactions would suggest. Why, how and what scale and quantities that happens is basically the study of complex systems. For example, a couple of people talking to each other, expressing their political beliefs (Micro-level) is a rather normal thing but if scaled, it can turn out to have varying and unpredictable effects (political turmoil, complex citizen-state interactions and conflict or revolution). How and to what degree that happens (a political revolution for example) can be analysed with the complexity science framework and the tools it offers. Political (or other) revolutions are particularly interesting because they are a prime example of a so-called social tipping point. A couple of people interacting with each other and organising together can lead towards a large-scale effect that can also cascade towards other countries or systems. Tipping points in all complex systems are very interesting to study because they tell us a lot about how these systems evolve (or emerge) which is important if we really want to understand the properties of the system. Also, interlinkages of different systems play a key role. As a trained economist this is something what is especially interesting to me because it gives us the ability to study the effects of political, social or ecological systems on the economic layer of the network. Complex System Studies are therefore inherently interdisciplinary if they want to study interlinkages. In traditional Economics these co-dependencies are usually ignored or somehow sidelined, which is not a very smart idea especially because we are able to model them in a rigorous way - but that is something mainstream economists continue to ignore or fail to understand. 

### What to do with it?
Given this sort of working-definition, I aim and am able to study a lot... My main interests are still green-transitions. As we work a lot on the modern design of the electricity grid at LIFE, I try working with [AMIRIS](https://gitlab.com/dlr-ve/esy/amiris) to simulate different pathways for a green-transition of the electricity system. AMIRIS can be an enormously huge Agent-Based-Model designed to analyse exactly these sort of things. The creators of the model worked for years on it, developed a whole java core and framework to process input and output as well as a python wrapper to execute scenarios and a python library to programmatically create scenarios to run to the core. But given the complicated structure of the agents interactions and the ways to set that up with .yaml files is hard and I am still working on figuring that out.  
If I had more time, I would also like to study financial markets. Why? I have some reasons. First, there is a whole stream in Economics called Econophysics that studies them using tools from statistical mechanics focusing on nonlinearities in the markets. So there is a lot of research to explore. Second, financial markets exhibit an enormous amount of data. I sometimes wonder whether we can infer important insights from the study of financial markets towards other domains where the available information is not as big. Maybe the study of financial markets as enormous information distribution systems shed light on the workings of markets in general?  
Another angle that is important for me is that they are very fragile and that has huge consequences on different layers of society. In fact, I believe that they are inherently unstable and follow some sort of cyclical pattern - a thought closely related to the work of [Hyman Minsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyman_Minsky).  These are some very broad - and possibly obscure - thoughts or starting points to conduct some research.
